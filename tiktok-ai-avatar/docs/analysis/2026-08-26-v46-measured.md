## 1. Is the skip rule real? — Era is NOT the confound. Something else is.

### 1a. The era-confound check passes, decisively and robustly

This was the right question to ask and the answer is favourable. Decomposing variance with era
as a binary factor:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| eta² (era → skip) | **0.299** — era explains 30% of skip variance, r = 0.547 |
| eta² (era → log views) | **0.023** — era explains 2% of view variance, r = 0.153 |
| confound-induced component `r(era,skip) x r(era,views)` | **0.084** |
| **partial corr(skip, log views \| era)** | **−0.775** |

**Controlling for era makes the correlation STRONGER, not weaker** (−0.725 → −0.775). Era acts as
a suppressor here: it moves skip a great deal and views barely at all, so the between-era component
was *diluting* the within-era relationship, not manufacturing it.

Sensitivity — the one input I had to estimate is SD(log₁₀ views), derived as 0.379 from
(median ≈ 240, 20% clear 500). Across the whole plausible range the conclusion does not move:

| SD(log₁₀ views) | 0.25 | 0.30 | **0.379** | 0.45 | 0.50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| partial corr | −0.735 | −0.754 | **−0.775** | −0.788 | −0.796 |

⭐⭐⭐ **The era confound that killed the ≥6s watch-time rule does not touch this one.** That is a
real, non-post-hoc result and it needed no threshold to obtain it. Record it.

### 1b. But the rule still is not real, for three reasons that have nothing to do with era

You checked the confound that was on record and it came back clean. The live objection is a
different one, and it is already in your own memory file, from the n=36 pass:

> "The correlation lives entirely in outliers: all-36 Spearman −0.452, but **restricted to the 29
> posts that never escaped the floor it is −0.056 — nothing**."

Nothing in tonight's n=60 data addresses that. Specifically:

1. **−0.725 is a two-cluster separation statistic wearing a correlation's clothes.** 12 winners at
   median 1,390 with low skip; 48 floor-dwellers at median ~210 with higher skip. A Pearson r over
   a two-blob point cloud reproduces the blob separation. It does **not** establish a gradient — and
   a gradient is the only thing you could act on ("cut skip by 0.05, get more views"). Within the
   blob this account actually lives in, the measured gradient is −0.056, i.e. zero.

2. **The direction of causation is unidentifiable from final-state data.** `media_reel_skip_rate` is
   a fraction *of the post's own views*, and views is the thing it is being used to predict. Two
   paths produce the same number: early hold earns distribution (the causal path you want), and
   final skip rate is an average over cohorts whose composition depends on how much distribution was
   granted (pure accounting). Your own memory already found the tell — **among the 7 winners skip
   RISES with size**, which is the accounting path's signature, not the causal one. Separating them
   needs the *first cohort's* skip rate, which Windsor does not expose.

3. **The 0.700 cut is threshold-shopping — with two knobs, not one.** The failed 0.690 rule was
   scored against 300 views; this one is 0.700 against 500. Both the predictor cut and the outcome
   cut were chosen after seeing the data. In fairness, the 12/18 vs 0/42 table is nominally so
   extreme (Fisher p ≈ 1e-8) that even a 40-comparison Bonferroni leaves p ≈ 4e-7, so the
   *separation* is not an artifact of shopping. But the separation is just §1b.1 restated. What
   shopping does buy is the false precision of "0.700" as a line, and the previous rule died on
   exactly that: 0.690 was the max of a winner set that had been silently defined by the cut.

### ⚖️ Verdict on Q1

**The association is real and it is not era. The RULE is not real.**

Say it in one line: **skip_rate is a good description of which posts won and a bad prediction of
which will win.** Use it as a post-hoc diagnostic and a floor to avoid (the surviving
`skip > 0.85 has never coincided with a winner`, p ≈ 0.01). Do not use it as a target, and do not
promote 0.700 to a gate. V46's 0.789 tells you V46 did not win. It does not tell you the hook was bad.

### 2b. Most of the "held better" is the runtime cut, not the hook

⭐⭐⭐ **The avg-watch-as-%-of-runtime row of the run table is invalid. V43/V44/V45 are 22.656s;
V46 is 12.352s. The denominator changed.** The first-second skill names this exact trap:
*"shortening improves the metric and does not improve the hook — never quote one as evidence for
the other."*

Correcting for it. Windsor's `avg_watch_ms` divides by viewers and includes replays (memory), so
divide back by views/viewers to get per-view seconds:

| | runtime | Windsor | views/viewers | **per-view sec** | % of runtime |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V45 | 22.656s | 3.087s | 1.22 | **2.52s** | 11.1% |
| V46 | 12.352s | 3.727s | 1.30 | **2.88s** | 23.3% |

**Absolute gain: +0.35 seconds.** The first-second skill already records that cutting 32s→12s
bought **0.4s by itself**. V46's entire watch-time gain is inside what shortening alone is
documented to buy. Independent check: integrating V46's own IG retention curve gives 2.40s per
view — consistent with the corrected 2.88s once replays are allowed, and nowhere near 3.727s.

⇒ **The watch-time numbers carry no evidence of a hook improvement.** Only the 1s hold and the skip
rate are runtime-independent, and therefore only those two could carry a hook signal at all.

### 2c. And on those two, V46 underperformed the null

This is the finding that matters, and the brief buries it by comparing V46 only to the three posts
immediately before it.

| baseline | skip | V46's delta |
|---|---|---|
| V45 alone | 0.879 | +0.090 better |
| V42–V45 median | 0.851 | +0.061 better |
| August mean | 0.801 | +0.012 better |
| **60-post mean** | **0.748** | **−0.041 WORSE** |

⭐⭐⭐ **If you knew nothing about V46, your best guess for its skip was the account mean, 0.748.

## 3. The 5 reposts

**They are not the best repost rate of the run. They are the same raw count as V43.**

| | reposts | reach | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| V43 | **5** | 162 | 3.1% |
| V46 | **5** | 125 | 4.0% |

Identical numerator. The rate is higher only because reach was lower — and "engagement rate with
reach in the denominator" is the metric family your memory retired precisely because it *rises when
a post loses distribution*. IG's "Higher" flag is computed on that same rate basis, so it inherits

**Poisson reality:** the 95% CI on a single observed count of 5 is **1.6 – 11.7**. Five reposts is
statistically indistinguishable from two, or from nine.

**On the mechanism:** reposts to Story are not sends. The only measured >30×-follower breakout
mechanism is a **send to a named person**, and Windsor's `media_shares` for V46 is **1**. V46 was
explicitly designed to enable that send ("you always know someone's NAME, you rarely know their
birthday") and it produced one event, indistinguishable from zero.

### ⚖️ Verdict on Q3

**Noise — but the instrument is worth keeping, read correctly.** See §5: low-base-rate *counts* are
far better powered than a proportion pinned near 0.8. Accumulate raw reposts and raw DM sends across
the arm and read the **sum**, never a per-post rate.

## 5. ⚖️ Verdict on the arm: CHANGE the read. Do not run 5 and read the median.

### 5a. The arm is underpowered for the effect it is chasing — by its own MDE table

| effect to detect | posts needed per arm |
|---|---|
| 0.115 (what n=5 buys) | 5 |
| **0.090** — V46 vs V45 | **8.2** |
| **0.062** — V46 vs the V42–V45 baseline | **17.3** |
| 0.041 — V46 vs the 60-post mean | 39.5 |

**The effect V46 actually produced is smaller than what 5 posts can resolve, against every baseline
you would plausibly use.** The arm will return "no significant difference" whether or not the change
works — and then someone will read the median anyway and call it. That is the n=1 failure being
rebuilt at n=5.

### 5b. Extending to 8–11 posts would work statistically and still not help

Eleven days is affordable, and within-arm era drift is tolerable (0.100/month ⇒ ~0.037 over 11 days,
under the n=11 MDE of 0.078 — though it eats half of it, so do not extend past ~2 weeks).

The problem is what you would own at the end: a reliable estimate that the V46 class cuts skip by
~0.08. **Per §1b, a 0.08 skip improvement among floor-dwelling posts predicts nothing about views**
(within-floor gradient −0.056). You would spend eleven days measuring a number that does not connect
to the outcome.

⭐⭐⭐ **Do not buy more sample for an instrument that is not wired to the business. Change the
outcome, not the sample size.**

### 5c. Counts beat proportions on this account — by an order of magnitude

Skip is a proportion pinned near 0.8 with cross-post SD 0.092: terrible power. Low-base-rate counts
are cheap to read because a real change is a large *multiplicative* change:

| instrument | base rate | posts to detect 2× | posts to detect 3× |
|---|---|---|---|
| skip_rate (0.090 shift) | — | **8** | — |
| reposts | ~5/post | **2.3** | 0.7 |
| DM sends | ~1/post | **11.4** | 3.7 |

⚠️ Caveat, stated so it is not misused: raw counts scale mechanically with reach. Over this run reach
spans only 125–166 (1.3×), so raw counts are usable here — but report reach alongside, and if reach
ever swings wide, model counts as Poisson **with reach as an offset**. That is not the retired
ratio-as-quality-score; it is the correct way to account for the sampling variance that a naive
ratio of two small numbers throws away.

### 5d. The recommendation

1. **Continue posting the V46 class.** It is the best-supported cut on both runtime-independent
   attention metrics, and there is no argument for returning to the birthdate-gate hook. Hold the
   package; keep `gold-a` and `push` out of the arm as specified.
2. **Abandon "read the arm at 5 posts on the median skip."** It cannot fire. Demote skip_rate to a
   descriptive covariate reported alongside each post, plus the one surviving gate: **skip > 0.85 is
   a floor to avoid.**
3. **Read the arm on accumulated raw counts — DM sends first, reposts second — summed over ≥10
   posts, plus follower count.** A send is the only mechanism with measured breakout evidence.
4. **Read views only against the account's own contemporaneous trend**, never against a fixed ~200
   line. The account is losing ~19%/month of distribution; any absolute view target manufactures a
   false negative within weeks.

### 5e. Numbers of posts, plainly

| question | posts needed |
|---|---|
| Did the V46 class cut skip by the amount it appeared to (0.090)? | **8** |
| Did it cut skip vs the full V42–V45 baseline (0.062)? | 17 |
| Did DM sends double (1 → 2/post)? | **11** |
| Did DM sends triple? | 4 |
| Did reposts double (5 → 10/post)? | **3** |
| Did the follower count respond? | **unreadable at any affordable n** — net movement is 0 across 4 posts and ±1 is baseline churn |

⭐⭐⭐ **The most consequential fact in tonight's data is not V46. It is that the account lost 19% of
its views and 25% of its viewers month over month.** That is an era shift in progress — the same
class of thing that confounded the ≥6s rule — and it is happening independent of every V-series
variable. Skip itself drifted 0.701 → 0.801 across the same boundary, a 0.100 move: **the same
magnitude as the effect an 11-post arm is powered to detect.** Any arm read across an era boundary
is confounded by construction. Keep arms short, or interleave them.
