## 3. ⚖️ The duration rule is re-derived for the third time

V54 shipped on a YouTube table: "≤15s averages 83.3% viewed, >15s averages
56.7%". That table is largely an arithmetic artefact. Average percentage viewed
is *watch seconds ÷ runtime*, and watch seconds is pinned near three — so a
shorter cut raises the percentage **without buying one extra second of anybody's
attention.**

The prospective test confirms it exactly. Every ratio improved and the thing the
ratios are made of got worse:

- share of runtime watched: IG 14.5% → **24.7%**, TikTok 12.6% → **16.0%** ✅
- completion: TikTok watched-full 1.8% → **2.2%** ✅
- absolute attention: TikTok 2.94s → **1.98s** ❌

**Re-derive a rule's PREMISE, not its wording** — third payout of that exact
lesson in three sessions. The premise here was a correlation whose denominator
was the variable being changed.

⇒ **Duration is retired as a lever.** It is not "shorter is better" and it is
not "longer is better"; it is that runtime does not touch attention on this
account, and any metric with runtime in the denominator will lie about that.
